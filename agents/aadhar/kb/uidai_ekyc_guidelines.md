# UIDAI KYC / e-KYC Guidelines (Reference Summary)
Source basis: UIDAI official guidance (uidai.gov.in). Paraphrased and
condensed for TraceChain's Aadhar Verification Agent knowledge base.

## Section 1: Accepted Aadhaar Formats

1.1 All forms of Aadhaar are equally valid proof of identity, with no
preference given to one form over another: the physical Aadhaar letter,
e-Aadhaar (downloaded PDF), mAadhaar (mobile app), Aadhaar Paperless
Offline e-KYC (signed XML), and Aadhaar PVC card.

1.2 Masked Aadhaar replaces the first 8 digits of the 12-digit Aadhaar
number with "xxxx-xxxx", leaving only the last 4 digits visible. It
contains the holder's name, address, gender, date of birth, photograph,
and masked mobile number/email.

1.3 A digitally signed XML file (Aadhaar Paperless Offline e-KYC) can be
generated by the Aadhaar holder and shared with a service provider along
with a Share Code, without needing to expose the internet-based Aadhaar
number directly.

## Section 2: Verification & Authenticity

2.1 An XML-based offline e-KYC document is digitally signed by UIDAI,
allowing the receiving service provider to cryptographically verify the
demographic contents and confirm authenticity, unlike a plain scanned
document image which cannot be verified for tampering by the recipient.

2.2 A Virtual ID (VID) is a temporary 16-digit number an Aadhaar holder
can generate in place of their actual Aadhaar number, valid for 24 hours
or 10 uses, for use in verifications without exposing the underlying
Aadhaar number.

2.3 Service providers who receive an Aadhaar XML or Share Code must not
share, publish, or display the file or its contents with any other
party.

## Section 3: Use in Financial KYC

3.1 Aadhaar may be used for eKYC to open bank accounts and access
financial services, including under amended anti-money-laundering
recordkeeping rules that permit voluntary use of Aadhaar-based
biometric eKYC for account opening.

3.2 Where Aadhaar is not used, Digital KYC performed via alternate
documents (e.g. voter ID, driving licence) follows a separate,
comparably rigorous verification procedure.

3.3 The same eKYC/Digital-KYC procedures extend to opening of demat
accounts and investment in mutual funds, indicating regulators treat
Aadhaar-based verification as an accepted standard across financial
products, not just banking specifically.

## Section 4: Practical Notes for Automated Verification

4.1 Because a masked or plain-image Aadhaar cannot be cryptographically
verified by an automated system the way a signed XML can, submissions
in image/PDF form carry inherently higher verification uncertainty than
a signed offline e-KYC XML.

4.2 A verifying system has no technical means to confirm the
authenticity of a plain image/scanned Aadhaar on its own; corroborating
checks (e.g. name/DOB match against other submitted documents) are
necessary to raise confidence.
